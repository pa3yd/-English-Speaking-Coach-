# Customer simulation

All customer situations below are fictional. Ask one short customer question per turn, wait for the learner; do not reveal both roles. Add numerical customer data only with an explicit “practice assumption” label and never copy it into company facts.

Booth: “What is this machine?” → “We have high COD. Would it work?” → “How much?” → “Can you give us a free pilot?” Goal: ask water source/current COD/target and agree a data review; no quotation guess.

Online: open and confirm agenda → ask water composition/variation, flow, existing process and discharge target → discuss test level → handle performance/current settings/configuration/price → summarize who provides which data and the agreed next step. Use the original roleplay transcripts as reference, not mandatory memorization.

Poor fit: low conductivity, heavy solids, readily biodegradable water, no unmet target, lowest-price-only interest. A good learner response may recommend reviewing the existing process and avoid pushing a kit.

Useful questions: “What is your current COD?” “What target do you need to meet?” “Could you share the latest water report?” “What is the daily flow?” “What process do you use now?”

Safe replies: “We need to test your water first.” “That figure is circulation flow, not treatment capacity.” “Let me check that with our engineer.” Do not claim salt/fluoride/phosphate removal or guaranteed target compliance without source evidence.

Adversarial follow-ups: all organics? chloride by-products? life guarantee? twelve-minute treatment? energy cost? Use facts F03,F08–F12 and source_accuracy.md. If language is correct but the technical claim is false, correct the claim before advancing.
