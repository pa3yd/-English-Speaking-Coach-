Boromond BDD Trade Show Role Play

A short booth conversation for Krystal Tan and a potential customer. Use it to ask useful questions, explain BDD simply, and agree on one next step.

Source basis: V8 PDF, 15 pages, and the Boromond Chinese catalogue dated 20260521 found beside the attachment. V8 PPTX and speaker notes were not available. Company statements below are source-supported statements, not independently verified performance. Scripts, customer dialogue and practice prompts are suggested language. [NEEDS INTERNAL CONFIRMATION] marks unresolved facts or terms and is not spoken aloud.

Practice setup

Fictional visitor and needs. No customer status or project outcome is implied. Take 4–6 minutes with a partner. Read the visitor prompt, look away, and answer in your own words. If the visitor is in a hurry, ask only about the main problem, current COD, target and contact for a data review; save the rest for a meeting.

Main conversation

Krystal: Hello. Welcome to Boromond. What brings you to the show?

Visitor: We need a better way to treat wastewater at our chemical plant.

Krystal: What is the main problem with your current process?

Visitor: Our COD is still above our target after biological treatment.

Krystal: What are the current COD and the target?

Visitor: I have the figures at the office, but not with me.

Krystal: No problem. What does the water contain? Any solvents or salts?

Visitor: There are solvents and chloride. What does BDD mean?

Krystal: It means boron-doped diamond. We use a thin, conductive diamond coating as an anode.

Visitor: How does it treat the water?

Krystal: Oxidation at the anode can break down organics. The water chemistry affects the result.

Visitor: So salt helps?

Krystal: It can improve conductivity. But chloride can also lead to chlorinated by-products. We need to evaluate that.

Visitor: What equipment do you sell?

Krystal: Electrode plates, test modules and kits, electrolyzers, and cabinet systems. Are you building a cell or treating water?

Visitor: We operate the treatment plant.

Krystal: Then we should first review your water. What is the daily flow and current treatment process?

Visitor: We need to confirm the flow. We use pretreatment and biology.

Krystal: Could your water-treatment engineer share the process flow and recent analysis?

Visitor: How much would a system cost?

Krystal: Price depends on the required anode area, power, and scope. We need data before a project quotation.

Visitor: Can you give us a free pilot unit?

Krystal: We do not provide free equipment or free pilot units. Testing arrangements are agreed case by case.

Visitor: Why do we need a water test?

Krystal: It helps us check the target, treatment time, and energy use on your water. A case result cannot predict yours.

Visitor: Could we test at our plant?

Krystal: PK3.0 is a portable kit for two-to-five-liter tests. Our engineer needs to confirm whether it fits your test plan.

Visitor: What should I send?

Krystal: COD, ammonia nitrogen, chloride, pH, daily flow, your target, and the current process. Please include your deadline.

Visitor: I'll ask our engineer to send the report after the show.

Krystal: Thank you. Who should join the technical review? Let's agree on a review time once the report is ready.

Visitor: Our treatment engineer and I will join. I'll confirm a time by email.

Krystal: Great. I'll wait for the report and your proposed time. Here is my contact information.

A visitor who may not be a good fit

Visitor: Our water has low conductivity. Most of the organics are easy to biodegrade, and we have a lot of solids.

Krystal: Those are reasons to be careful about BDD. Is there a specific target your existing process cannot meet?

Visitor: No. We mainly want the lowest treatment cost.

Krystal: Then BDD may not be the right starting point. Your treatment engineer should review the current process first.

Visitor: Should we buy a testing kit anyway?

Krystal: I would not recommend equipment from this information. If you later identify a difficult side stream, we can review its data.

Likely follow up questions

Visitor: Can it remove every organic pollutant?

Suggested answer: We cannot promise that. We need to test the specific water and target.

Visitor: Will it remove all the salt?

Suggested answer: We should not present BDD as a desalination process. Let our engineer review your salt-related target.

Visitor: How long do the electrodes last?

Suggested answer: Service life depends on the duty. We need the operating conditions before confirming a project-specific expectation.

Visitor: Can we buy only the plates?

Suggested answer: Yes, V8 offers plates for cell builders and OEM partners. Please share your cell design and coating requirements.

Visitor: Can we skip the small kit?

Suggested answer: Possibly. The route follows the available data. You do not need to buy every stage.

Visitor: Will the water look clear?

Suggested answer: Color is only one observation. We must check the agreed treatment indicators.

Visitor: Is it cheaper than our current process?

Suggested answer: We need comparable treatment and cost data. I cannot confirm savings from the information we have.

Visitor: Can you ship next week?

Suggested answer: Let me confirm the configuration and delivery schedule internally. [NEEDS INTERNAL CONFIRMATION]

If you get stuck

Could you say that more slowly?

Let me confirm your main treatment target.

I do not have a confirmed figure. Let me check with our engineer.

Could we agree on a data review as the next step?

Finish with a concrete action

Use a real name and an agreed date only after the visitor confirms them. Practice: “You will send the water report by [agreed date]. I will coordinate a technical review after we receive it. We will then confirm whether testing is appropriate.” Do not promise a free test, sample acceptance or a quotation deadline without internal confirmation.

Memory route: Greet → ask about water → clarify target → explain simply → qualify → agree next action.
